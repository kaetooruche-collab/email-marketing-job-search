## Method (audit-first, not service-first)

Don't pitch "email marketing services." Find a business with an **observable, specific gap**, name it precisely, and open with the gap — not the pitch.

Qualifying signal checklist (need at least one, concrete and visible, not assumed):
- Has a website/social presence collecting emails but no visible newsletter or follow-up sequence
- Runs webinars/events with no visible registration-to-attendance email flow
- E-commerce store with no visible abandoned-cart or post-purchase sequence
- Newsletter exists but is discount-only / low-effort (no lifecycle structure)
- Advertises a lead magnet with no visible nurture after opt-in

First message principle: point at the one gap, propose the smallest fix, no agency pitch, no multi-service menu. E.g. "I noticed [X] — I'd set up a 3-email flow to [specific outcome] instead." This matches master prompt §12.3 ("one primary offer per prospect," "don't send a generic list of every service").

Channel priority, per master prompt §12/§7.3:
1. Warm introduction (person you or the business owner already trusts) — highest conversion, skips credibility-from-zero problem
2. Direct, specific, personalized outreach to an identified decision-maker with an identified gap — never mass/generic
3. Platform-based cold outreach (LinkedIn 1st-degree connections who run businesses) — only after 1 and 2 are exhausted

Explicitly ruled out per master prompt §2.1/§8.2/§18: free full audits/strategies as a sales tactic, substantial unpaid trial work, mass unsolicited DMs, scraping private contact info.

### Why generic search is running out of road

Google-style phrase search ("solo [niche] website book a free call") increasingly returns: (a) marketing/business-coaching content aimed AT practitioners rather than practitioner sites themselves, (b) course-sellers and practice-growth consultants, (c) directory aggregator pages with no individual site behind them. The deeper into a niche a search goes, the more these crowd out real small-practice sites.

### Better discovery channels to use instead, going forward

1. **Professional-association "find a practitioner" directories** — these list real, individually verified practitioners by design, often filterable by solo/independent status or location:
   - Nutritional Therapy Association (NTA) practitioner directory
   - British Association for Nutritional Therapy (BANT) directory
   - DONA International doula directory
   - ILCA ("Find a Lactation Consultant") directory
   - Psychology Today's therapist directory (filterable by state, and profiles often self-describe as solo/group)
   - State licensing board public directories (confirms licensure; doesn't confirm web presence, so pair with a follow-up site check)
2. **Press/podcast-mention search** — "[niche] featured in [local outlet]" or searching outlet names directly (Voyage ATL, ShoutOut, CanvasRebel-style city magazines, local news segments) surfaced two of the six qualified prospects so far (Susan Robinson, Angela Fishman) — a notably higher hit rate than generic phrase search.
3. **Instagram bio-link browsing** — searching niche + "solo" directly on Instagram (not Google) surfaced Reclaiming Her Counseling; the bio often states solo/group status explicitly and links to the live current site, avoiding the expired-domain problem hit this batch.
4. **Local business directory listings (not aggregator SEO content)** — e.g. a city's own small-business spotlight features, chamber of commerce member listings.

### Refinement after batch 4, round 1 (2026-10-30): press-mention search has a real tension

Batch 4's first round checked 4 press-featured practitioners found via the method in point 2 above — all 4 had press coverage (Sacramento Magazine, Forbes Health, U.S. News, MindBodyGreen, etc.) and all 4 already had an active email opt-in or list tool (Flodesk, a "subscribe" CTA, etc.). Zero qualified. The pattern: strong, multi-outlet press coverage correlates with marketing sophistication generally — the ambition and resources that earn someone a Forbes Health feature usually also got them a newsletter. Press-mention search still finds genuinely well-researched specific detail for a good opener, but it's biased toward people who already have the thing being pitched. Don't treat a press hit alone as a qualifying signal — it narrows the "interesting person" search, not the "has a gap" search. Still check for the gap independently before investing in a draft.

Also newly confirmed as a real disqualifying pattern: **lactation consultants and IBCLCs skew toward already having an opt-in or course-funnel** (3 of 3 checked so far — Annie Frisbie, Katie James, Stephanie Wong — either had multi-person practices or active email tools). Downgrading this sub-niche's priority for future batches unless a specific candidate shows no opt-in on direct inspection.

### Per-niche checklist before spending a scrape on a candidate

1. Does the "About" or bio explicitly say solo/founder, or name 2+ practitioners/staff? (biggest single disqualifier)
2. Is there already a visible opt-in, lead magnet, or "subscribe to our emails" block anywhere? (second biggest disqualifier)
3. Is the platform Shopify/product-brand-shaped rather than a service-booking site? (correlates strongly with already-has-capture)
4. Is there real audience-building evidence (press, podcast, book, YouTube) to anchor a specific, non-generic opener? (not required, but raises message quality when present — true for 4 of the 6 qualified prospects so far)

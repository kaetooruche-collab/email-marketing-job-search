1. How to use this document
Treat sections 3 to 5 as the only approved source of claims. If a claim is not in the claim bank, do not write it.
Every claim carries an evidence grade (section 3). Never upgrade a grade, never round a number up, and keep each figure’s scope attached (period, population, who measured it).
Section 2 lists hard rules that override any style or persuasion goal. Section 6 lists what must never appear.
Where this document says “confirm with KT”, ask or flag it. Do not guess.
Provenance: grade A figures were re-counted from the Brevo exports (see section 5A); other claims were stated by KT and are not independently verified. Wording marked [inference] is the author’s reading.
2. Hard rules
Name: use “Kaeto Oruche” on portfolio material. Not the full legal name, and not “KT”, which is an internal shorthand.
Location line on CVs: “Kyrenia, Cyprus”. Do not tailor the location to a target city.
Pharmacy credential: KT is not a licensed or registered pharmacist. Never write “pharmacist”, “licensed pharmacist”, “registered pharmacist” or “as a pharmacist”. Use “MPharm graduate”, “pharmacy graduate”, “trained in pharmacy” or “worked in community pharmacy”.
Employer naming: in public-facing material (portfolio site, Upwork profile and bids, pitches, LinkedIn) do not name HIBARR. Describe it as “a German-market financial advisory company”. Do not call it a “brand”. On CVs, follow KT’s instruction for that application (the Canadian-format pharmacy CV is to name HIBARR Trading Limited). If unsure, default to the generic description and ask.
Engagement metrics: open rates and click rates are below benchmark and must not appear on a CV. Defensible headline metrics are complaint rate, unsubscribe trajectory and raw volume (use the re-counted figures in section 5).
Audience: do not state an age range for HIBARR’s audience. It has not been confirmed.
Certificates: the De Montfort University certificate stays off all CVs and letters.
Human voice: CVs and bids must read as written by a person (see section 8).
Honesty over polish: stretch jobs are worth trying, but never manufacture equivalence where responsibility, technical depth, regulation or seniority differ.

7. Use-case guidance
7.1 CV
Lead with C1, C2, C3, C4, then the systems claims (C7, C8, C10, C11). Close with C14 and C23.
Format for Canadian or other pharmacy roles: a plain, human-written CV that does not read as city-specific. Name the employer only as section 2 allows.
Judge every role by work performed, ownership and problem complexity. Title and years alone mislead.
Jobs requiring more than about two years of experience are outside KT’s search. His stated cutoff was tightened from 3 years to “anything above 2 years disqualifies”; the exact treatment of 2 years was not clarified.
7.2 Portfolio site
Name the employer generically. Present three or four case studies built from C4 (hygiene and unsubscribe trajectory), C10 (lifecycle model and why opens were demoted), C11 (list cleanup) and C12 (webinar email production).
Show structure and reasoning, not engagement results. Each case study should state the problem, the decision, and what is and is not known about the outcome.
Include the soft bounce weak spot only in a short “what I would fix next” note if KT wants it.
7.3 Pitch (cold outreach to a prospective client or employer)
Open with the recipient’s likely problem, not KT’s history. Use one or two claims only, chosen for relevance (C4 and C11 for list health; C10 for lifecycle design; C15 for deliverability).
End with one specific question about their list or sending setup. No attachments, no promises of results.
For health companies (the first freelance target segment KT chose), the MPharm and community pharmacy background is a differentiator. Use the section 2 phrasing.
7.4 Upwork bid
Length: short. Three to six sentences plus one question.
Structure: (1) restate the client’s problem in their words; (2) one relevant proof point from the claim bank; (3) one concrete first step KT would take; (4) one question about their setup.
Platforms: name Brevo confidently. For Klaviyo, Mailchimp or HubSpot jobs, say what KT has actually done and do not imply platform experience he lacks.
Never quote an outcome for the client. Never cite open or click rates.
Profile headline direction: lifecycle email and CRM systems for regulated or health-adjacent businesses. KT has said he expects to lean into copywriting, so copy samples may be added to the portfolio later; none are documented here.
HIBARR permits outside client work, so freelance engagement does not conflict with the current role.
8. Wording and tone rules
Plain, concrete, specific. Numbers with scope, then stop.
CV bullets start with a verb and carry no pronouns. Bids and pitches are written in first person.
Avoid common AI tells: “passionate”, “results-driven”, “spearheaded”, “leveraged”, “seamlessly”, “delve”, “tapestry”, “in today’s fast-paced”, stacked triplets, and em-dash-heavy sentences.
Vary sentence length. Do not open every bullet the same way.
Use “designed” or “built” for B-grade claims. Use “reduced” or “held” only for the A-grade results.
If a draft needs a claim that is not in section 5, stop and flag the gap rather than filling it.

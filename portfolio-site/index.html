Case Study

The 216-Campaign Forensic Audit

216 is the number of historical campaigns audited — distinct from the ~230 campaigns run January–August 2026 under the resulting framework.

Business challenge

A financial advisory company serving a German-market audience had run hundreds of weekly campaigns across banking and property verticals with no systematic framework for what to send.

Underlying problem

Content was inconsistent, angles repeated without anyone tracking it, and there was no way to know which topics actually moved engagement — every send started from a blank page.

Investigation

Audited all 216 past campaigns, tagging each by topic, angle, vertical and engagement result, to find out which angles were being overused and which hadn't been tried.

System built

A three-tier content framework ranked by engagement, a set of distinct content angles derived from the findings, and a library of 20 reusable email frameworks with rotation rules so no contact sees the same approach twice in a row.

Why it matters

Content selection moved from "what feels relevant this week" to a structured, trackable rotation — the kind of operating system a team can run without reinventing the plan every Monday.

Case Study

The Webinar Email Engine

A live, weekly-recurring campaign system, still running.

Business challenge

The company runs a weekly webinar for its German-market audience as its primary conversion event. The database includes people who've never registered, people who registered but never attended, and past attendees — three populations with very different relationships to the brand.

Underlying problem

Early campaigns treated the database uniformly. Engagement in the past-attendee segment decayed badly under repeated, undifferentiated weekly sends.

System built

A three-tier invite system with different cadence and copy treatment per tier, produced weekly, plus a locked and stress-tested post-webinar template. Mandatory formula rotation means no contact gets the same approach twice running.

Why it matters

This is the difference between a newsletter and a system: the same weekly event, but three different conversations depending on what each contact has actually done.

What's not yet quantified: I haven't separately measured the performance delta the tiered system produced versus the uniform approach it replaced — the system is live and in its stress-testing phase.
Case Study

The Scandinavian Re-Engagement Sequence

Business challenge

Sales was about to cold-call 481 Scandinavian leads who'd gone quiet — outreach to fully disengaged contacts has a predictable failure rate.

System built

A three-email sequence over ten days, segmented by engagement tier, each email doing one job: re-establish context, provide value, create a reason to respond before the call.

Honestly: no reply or conversion data was separately tracked for this sequence. What I can show is the architecture and the reasoning — a deliberate warm-up layer instead of handing sales a cold list of names.
Systems I've Built

Sole owner of the email marketing function for a financial advisory company serving a German-market audience, since January 2026 — built from scratch on Brevo.

Architecture

Lifecycle State Model

A mutually exclusive state system where every contact sits in exactly one current state, while sticky history flags track everything they've ever done. A resolver computes state automatically from precedence logic.

Qualification

Evidence-Led Lead Scoring

Rebuilt lead scoring from a 40-point to a 20-point scale using 200+ historical campaigns as the evidence base, with a dual-gate qualification rule rather than a single threshold.

Engagement

Behaviour-Based Segmentation

Five tiers driven by click recency, not opens — Apple Mail Privacy Protection makes open data unreliable, so the model is built around the signal that's still trustworthy.

Attribution

UTM Infrastructure

A tracking structure covering 750+ tagged URLs across 5 platforms, with standardised naming so every click attributes to its original source, medium and campaign.

Hygiene

MX pre-filtering on inbound leads across a database of roughly 85,000 contacts, with orphaned-record isolation (1,564 records found and remediated) and engagement-based suppression.

How I Approach Email Marketing

Accelerated conversion vs. long-term nurture

The core idea behind the systems above: not every contact needs the same message at the same cadence. Someone who's demonstrated high intent needs a short, direct path to conversion. Someone still building trust needs education, not a sales pitch repeated weekly. Treating both the same way is the most common mistake in email programmes that rely on a single broadcast calendar.

⚡ Accelerated track

"The Microwave"

High-intent leads who've demonstrated enough engagement that generic educational content makes little sense. Shorter path, more direct communication, accelerated toward conversion.

🔥 Nurture track

"The Oven"

Contacts who need weeks or months of education before they have enough trust or urgency. You don't keep opening the door every five minutes asking why they're not ready — you let the process work.

Permission marketing is the privilege — not the right — of delivering anticipated, personal and relevant messages to people who actually want to get them.Seth Godin
